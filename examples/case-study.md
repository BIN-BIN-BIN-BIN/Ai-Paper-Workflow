# Case study: a qualitative cultural heritage paper from existing field materials

> **Illustrative example.** The researcher, troupe, place and all materials below are fictional. The exchange is abridged to show how the skill behaves: how it routes the study, how gates block progress, and what the Paper Progress Card looks like.

## Setting

Dr. Lin studies a (fictional) shadow-puppetry troupe in Hexi County. She already has:

- 16 semi-structured interviews with puppeteers (transcribed, Chinese)
- 3 years of field notes from rehearsals and temple-fair performances
- about 300 photos of puppets and stage set-ups
- no plan yet for a paper

She opens a new conversation in her AI assistant (any tool that supports Agent Skills).

---

## Stage 0 — inventory, route, paradigm

**User**

> Use ai-paper-workflow. I have 16 interviews with shadow puppeteers, three years of field notes and ~300 photos. I want to turn this into a journal paper as fast as possible.

**Assistant (abridged)**

> **Judgement:** this fits the *existing materials* route, and the materials point to a *qualitative* study. The Cultural Heritage Module applies.
>
> Candidate questions the materials could answer:
>
> | # | Question | Evidence needed | Novelty |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | How do puppeteers decide which traditional plays to adapt for younger audiences? | Interviews + rehearsal notes | High |
> | 2 | How is puppet-carving knowledge passed on outside formal apprenticeship? | Interviews + photos | Medium |
> | 3 | Has attendance changed since 2020? | Ticket or attendance records (**not in hand**) | — |
>
> Question 3 would need quantitative data you do not have, so I would set it aside.
>
> **Gate (Stage 0)**
> ✔ Route: existing materials
> ✔ Paradigm: qualitative
> ✘ Rights status of the 300 photos is not recorded (Heritage Module). Please list the photographer and permission for each batch before we use any images.

---

## Stage 1 — the gate blocks a weak contribution

**User**

> Question 1. Contribution: "This is the first study to describe how the Hexi troupe adapts plays."

**Assistant (abridged)**

> **Judgement:** this does not pass the gate yet. "First to describe" is a gap in coverage, not a contribution. A reviewer will ask: *what do we now understand that we did not before?*
>
> A stronger form: "Studies of heritage adaptation focus on state programmes and festivals; they say little about the decisions performers make in rehearsal. Using interviews and three years of rehearsal observation, this study shows how puppeteers negotiate between ritual obligations and audience expectations, and proposes a three-type model of adaptation."
>
> **Gate (Stage 1)**
> ✔ Target journal and backup chosen
> ✘ Contribution type stated (new model / interpretation), pending your confirmation that the data can support a typology

---

## Stage 2 — blank analysis framework instead of dummy tables

Because the study is qualitative, the skill builds a **blank analysis framework**, not blank statistical tables.

**Initial coding frame (excerpt)**

| Code | Definition | Example excerpt (to fill) |
|---|---|---|
| Ritual obligation | References to plays or scenes that must be kept for religious reasons | |
| Audience pull | References to changing a play because of audience reaction | |
| Craft constraint | Changes driven by puppet design, stage size or staffing | |

**Case × theme matrix (headers only)**

| Puppeteer | Ritual obligation | Audience pull | Craft constraint | Adaptation type |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Evidence-chain table (headers only)**

| Claim | Excerpt ID | Source type (oral / document / researcher interpretation) |
|---|---|---|

**Assistant: gate check (Stage 2)**

> General
> ✔ Each research question maps to a matrix or table that would answer it
>
> Qualitative
> ✔ Tradition: reflexive thematic analysis
> ✔ Sampling: purposive (all active puppeteers, 16 of 19)
> ✔ Sample-size rationale: information power (narrow aim, dense dialogue, specific sample)
> ✔ Quality strategies: negative-case analysis; member checking with two senior puppeteers
> ✘ Positionality statement not drafted (you are a long-term volunteer with the troupe; this needs stating)
>
> Cultural Heritage Module
> ✘ Consent covers publication but **not uploading transcripts to AI tools**. Before using AI-assisted coding, confirm this with participants or de-identify the transcripts.
> ✘ Some rehearsal scenes involve a ritual opening that elders asked not to film. Write a handling rule for this material.
>
> **Not ready for Stage 3.** Three items are missing; all belong to this stage.

**Paper Progress Card**

```
[Paper Progress Card]
Working title: Negotiating ritual and audience: adaptation decisions in a shadow-puppetry troupe
Route: existing materials
Paradigm: qualitative
Qualitative tradition: reflexive thematic analysis
Cultural heritage module: yes
Target journal / backup journal: (chosen in Stage 1)
One-sentence contribution statement: (revised version from Stage 1)
Current stage: Stage 2
Gate status: ✔ coding frame, sampling, quality strategies / ✘ positionality; AI-use consent; ritual-material rule
Outputs completed: material inventory; contribution statement; blank analysis framework
Next step: draft positionality statement; confirm AI-use consent or de-identify; write ritual-material rule
```

Dr. Lin saves the card. Next week she pastes it into a new conversation, and the skill continues from Stage 2.

## Variant: turning it into a mixed-methods study

Suppose Dr. Lin also runs a survey of 120 audience members. The skill would ask:

- What does **integration** add that neither strand gives alone? (e.g. whether performers' beliefs about audience preferences match what audiences report)
- Which design? Here, *convergent*: both strands analysed separately, then compared.
- Then it builds a **blank joint display**:

| Topic | Survey result | Interview finding | Relationship | Meta-inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preference for shortened plays | | | confirm / complement / conflict | |

If the two strands conflict, the skill requires the paper to report and explain the conflict rather than only the agreements.
